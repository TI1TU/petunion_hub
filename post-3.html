5 Basic Commands Every Dog Should Know

Start your dog's training journey with these five essential commands that ensure safety, reliable communication, and good manners in any environment.

Sarah Jones Professional Canine Behavior Trainer

  • 2. The Lifesaver: 'Come' (Recall)

  • 5. The Safety Net: 'Leave It'

Dog training is not about performing circus tricks—it is about opening a two-way channel of communication that keeps your dog safe, confident, and welcome anywhere in public.

1. The Foundation: 'Sit'

Sit is the default polite behavior. Use treat-luring: hold a savory treat right at your dog's nose, then slowly sweep your hand upward over their head. As their nose tilts up, their rear naturally lowers. The instant their bottom meets the floor, mark with 'Yes!' and reward.

2. The Lifesaver: 'Come' (Recall)

A rock-solid recall can literally save your dog's life if they slip a collar near a busy intersection. Never use 'Come' to summon your dog for something unpleasant (like nail trims or ear drops). Always make coming to you the most rewarding party in town.

"Your voice when calling 'Come' should be the happiest invitation your dog has ever heard. Never punish a dog when they finally return to you, no matter how long it took."

3. The Self-Control Pillar: 'Stay'

Once your dog understands Sit, open your flat palm toward them and say 'Stay'. Take one step back. If they remain still for two seconds, step back in, mark, and reward. Gradually build the three D's: Duration, Distance, and Distraction.

4. The Settling Command: 'Down'

Lie down is an inherently calming posture that lowers excitement. From a Sit position, lure the treat straight down between their front paws and slide it forward along the ground like an L-shape until their elbows touch down.

5. The Safety Net: 'Leave It'

From chicken bones on sidewalks to dropped human medications, 'Leave It' teaches impulse control. Present a closed fist with a treat. When your dog stops pawing or licking your hand and pulls back, immediately reward with a higher-value treat from your opposite hand.

The Five-Minute Golden Rule

Dogs learn best in short, energetic 3 to 5 minute sessions rather than grueling hour-long marathons. Always conclude each training drill on a winning note with praise and play.
